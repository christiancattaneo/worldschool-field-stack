# Source Evaluation Rubric

Use this rubric on every source you cite. The Skeptic subagent applies it automatically; you also apply it by hand at least three times during Week 3.

Score each source on five dimensions, 1–10. Total possible: 50.

## 1. Authority (1–10)

Who wrote this and what makes them credible on this topic?

- 10: A leading researcher or institution in the specific field
- 7:  A credentialed practitioner or recognized journalist
- 4:  A general writer or commentator without specific credentials
- 1:  Anonymous, AI-generated, or unverifiable

## 2. Currency (1–10)

When was this published, and does the field move fast enough that age matters?

- 10: Published in the last 2 years on a fast-moving topic; or a foundational older paper on a slow-moving topic
- 7:  3–5 years old, still relevant
- 4:  6–10 years old; reference value, but field has moved
- 1:  Decades old in a fast-moving field; or no date listed

## 3. Methodology (1–10)

How did the author know what they're claiming?

- 10: Primary research with stated methods, sample, and limitations
- 7:  Synthesis citing multiple primary sources transparently
- 4:  Opinion supported by anecdote
- 1:  Assertion with no supporting reasoning or evidence

## 4. Bias / Funding (1–10)

What's the author's stake in the conclusion? Who paid for it?

- 10: Independent funding, transparent disclosure of any conflicts
- 7:  Mild stake but disclosed (e.g., academic with consulting work)
- 4:  Funded by an interested party; disclosed
- 1:  Funded by an interested party and not disclosed; or strong ideological framing without disclosure

## 5. Primary vs. Secondary (1–10)

Is this the original source or a summary of one?

- 10: Primary source — the actual study, interview, document, fieldnote
- 7:  High-quality secondary source citing primaries you can trace
- 4:  Tertiary source (a textbook, encyclopedia article)
- 1:  Quaternary or further removed; or paraphrase with no citation

## Total + Action

| Total | What to do |
|-------|------------|
| 40–50 | Cite confidently; this is a foundation source |
| 30–39 | Cite, but note the weakest dimension and look for stronger |
| 20–29 | Use as a lead, not a citation; find the primary source it points to |
| Below 20 | Discard. Do not cite. |

## When AI Paraphrases Beyond the Source

If your AI tool (Perplexity, Claude, Comet) produces a claim, locate the original cited source and read enough of it to verify the AI's paraphrase is accurate. Common failure modes:

- The AI extends a narrow finding into a broader one
- The AI attributes a claim to a paper that does not contain it
- The AI confuses two similar-sounding studies

If you find an AI paraphrase has gone beyond what the source supports, note it in your journal and Slack the cohort. Catching these is the research move.
